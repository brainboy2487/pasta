the field of higher-dimensional Markov space is real as a concept in mathematics and statistics, primarily studied under the name of Markov random fields (MRFs) or multidimensional Markov chains. These are extensively used in various scientific and engineering applications. 
The Concept in Mathematics and Science
Generalization of Markov Property: A standard Markov chain applies the "memoryless" property in a one-dimensional sequence (usually time). A Markov random field extends this property to systems with a multi-dimensional index set, such as a grid in 2D (image processing) or 3D (geostatistics, physical systems).
Theoretical Foundation: The properties of these higher-dimensional spaces are rigorously investigated using theoretical reasoning and calculation, even if a direct physical example isn't immediately obvious. The mathematics of high-dimensional spaces is a well-established area of study, often dealing with large numbers of variables or features.
Terminology: The term "higher dimensional Markov space" is a general description for the study of these phenomena. The formal academic term is typically "Markov random field" or "multidimensional Markov model". 
Real-World Applications
These models are "real" in the sense that they provide powerful frameworks for modeling complex, real-world phenomena: 
Image Processing and Computer Vision: MRFs are foundational in tasks like image segmentation, texture analysis, and computer vision, where dependencies exist between neighboring pixels.
Geostatistics: They are used in modeling multidimensional field predictions, such as subsurface properties for geological surveys.
Statistical Physics: Models of disordered systems, such as the Ising model, are a prototypical example of Markov random fields used in statistical mechanics.
Machine Learning and Data Mining: Researchers use high-dimensional Markov models to tackle challenges in complex datasets like genomics and finance, or for developing efficient Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling methods in high dimensions. 
In conclusion, the study and application of the Markov property in high-dimensional spaces are a very active and practical field of research and application.
